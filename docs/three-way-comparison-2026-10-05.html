How it was tested

The thirteen homes from the bonkers.ie report: small, typical and large; working from home; night-heavy; heat pump; storage heaters; an EV charged on a night timer and one at teatime; 4 kWp solar with and without a 10 kWh battery; rural; and an older 24-hour meter. Each is a year of half-hourly ESB readings with a known true cost for every plan. Each went through each site twice, typed and with the meter file, giving each site the answers it asks for (kilowatt.ie: EV kWh a night, solar size, battery size, heat-pump floor area). 75 runs in all.

Error is the median gap between a site's yearly cost and the true cost across every plan it prices. Cost is what following its top plan would cost against the truly cheapest. kilowatt.ie's figures have its €12.43 levy overcharge removed (see below); without that adjustment it is about 0.8% higher everywhere.

One disputed price decides most of the file results. From 12 October Energia raises its standard rates. kilowatt.ie and bonkers.ie both show its discounts cut at the same time (Smart 24 Hour 30% → 28%, Smart Data 27% → 23%); Energia's own plan page still shows 30% and 27%, and its tariff page gives only the new standard rates. Peakless follows Energia's page, which keeps Energia Smart 24 Hour the cheapest plan for ordinary homes. If the two comparison sites are right, Flogas Smart 24hr is cheapest instead: kilowatt.ie's file results become 11 of 12 and bonkers.ie's 11 of 12, and Peakless's become 6 of 12, each miss about €27–31 a year. That is the single most important thing to confirm.

Where each one goes wrong

kilowatt.ie

  • Last year's PSO levy. It charges €19.10; the levy fell to €6.67 on 1 October. Every quote is €12.43 too high. Its methodology page states the old figure.
  • Typed usage leans to night rates. Its typical home puts 32% of use at night (ESB's standard profile: 23%), so it recommends Electric Ireland's day/night plan to most homes, €33–81 a year off for typical, large and working-from-home homes.
  • EVs are assumed to charge in the cheapest hours. A car charged at teatime was sent to an EV night plan: €302 a year more. With the file it read the home correctly.
  • No storage-heater option: typed, a storage home is 19% out.
  • Solar typed in needs the home's total use, which a solar owner's bill does not show; even given it, its weather-based solar model gave costs 23% from this test's, so the solar figures depend heavily on whose solar model is nearer the truth.
  • Battery homes: assumes a timer charging from the grid at night and recommends an EV plan (€184 a year off for a battery that only takes solar). Peakless does the same when typed, but now says so on screen.
  • Plans a switcher cannot take sit in the ranking: Bord Gáis "Existing Customer" plans, SSE plans for smart meters with no signal, prepay and a "Winter/Summer split" scenario. Its number one for the solar-and-battery home was the split scenario.
  • SSE's main day/night/peak plan is missing.

bonkers.ie

  • As in the earlier report: a Nightsaver profile for every typed smart home, EVs assumed overnight, misread EV meter files (15% out), only 15 plans after an upload, and a false "incomplete data" warning.

Peakless

  • A meter file is averaged, not priced half-hour by half-hour. 0.2–1.2% out where the other two are exact. It has not changed a recommendation in these tests, but it would matter for weekend and dynamic plans.
  • Heat pump typed: 4.7% out and €38 off, against kilowatt.ie's 2.8% (kilowatt.ie models heat pumps from temperature).
  • Solar with a battery typed: recommends an EV plan on the assumption of grid charging (€147), now flagged on screen with the answer without it.

What would close Peakless's gaps

  • Confirm Energia's discounts from 12 October. Two independent sites say they fall; if so, Peakless's top pick for ordinary homes changes.
  • Price a meter file half-hour by half-hour, as both rivals do, instead of averaging it by month.
  • Model heat pumps from temperature, as kilowatt.ie does, for homes that type their usage.

Limits

  • The homes are synthetic, built from ESB's published profiles; heat pump, storage, solar and battery behaviour are this test's own assumptions, so typed results for those homes are the least certain.
  • The true cost uses each supplier's published prices as Peakless holds them. Where a site holds a different price (Energia from 12 October; Ecopower; Community Power's export rate; kilowatt.ie's Flogas day/night plan), its error here partly reflects that disagreement rather than its model.
  • Gas, prepay and dynamic plans were not scored.

Run 5 October 2026 through each site's public comparison page in a browser, one session at a time with pauses. kilowatt.ie's robots file allows its comparison pages; bonkers.ie's data API, which its robots file bars, was not used. Homes, true costs, scripts and raw results are kept with the Peakless repository.
