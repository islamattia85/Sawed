How it was tested

Thirteen homes, each a full year of half-hourly meter readings in ESB Networks' download format: small, typical and large homes; two people working from home; a night-heavy home; a heat pump; night storage heaters; an EV charged on a 2–5am timer and one charged at teatime; 4 kWp solar with and without a 10 kWh battery; a rural home; and a home on an older 24-hour meter. They are built from ESB's published standard profiles, with heating, cars, panels and batteries added hour by hour.

The true cost of every plan over the next 12 months was then worked out exactly: each half hour at the supplier's published rate, announced price changes from their date, export paid at each plan's export rate, the urban or rural standing charge and the €6.67 PSO levy.

Each home went through both sites twice: typing its yearly kWh (and its export, car, heating and panels where the site asks), and uploading its meter file. 50 runs in all: 25 on bonkers.ie through its public form, one at a time, and 25 on Peakless.

Scored on two things: how far each site's yearly cost was from the truth, averaged over every plan it priced, and what following its number-one plan would cost against the truly cheapest plan.

Where each one goes wrong

bonkers.ie

  • Typed usage assumes a night-heavy home. bonkers.ie prices every smart-meter home on ESB's Nightsaver profile (37% of use at night). Ordinary homes use 23% at night, so night-rate plans look 3–11% cheaper than they are, and it recommends Electric Ireland's day/night plan to almost everyone. Fine for the night-heavy and storage homes; €33–81 a year wrong for typical, large and working-from-home homes.
  • "Optimise for EV" assumes overnight charging. A car charged at teatime was steered onto an EV night plan: €302 a year more than the cheapest. Even with the meter file it recommended a night-rate plan for that home (€173).
  • Meter files from EV homes are misread. With the file, EV plans were priced 13–14% too cheap on average (up to 26%). Every file was flagged "incomplete" and topped up with estimates, though each held a full year.
  • Only 15 plans after a file upload; "show more" does not load the rest.
  • Storage heaters, typed: 14% average error (up to 42%): one split for every home cannot fit a home with most of its use overnight.

Peakless

  • Heat pumps and storage heaters, typed: 5% and 8% out. Peakless has its own shape for these homes, and so does this test; neither is measured, so this gap is uncertain. With the meter file both are within 0.2–1.2%.
  • Solar with a battery, typed: recommends an EV plan (€147 a year off for this home) because it assumes the battery can top up from the grid at night. That is advice to change a setting, not an error in the sums, but the screen should say so plainly.
  • Everything else, typed or file: the cheapest plan, within 1%.

Limits

  • The homes are synthetic, built from ESB's published profiles. The heat pump and storage-heater shapes, and the solar and battery behaviour, are this test's own assumptions, so typed-usage results for those homes are less certain than the rest.
  • The true cost uses each supplier's published prices as Peakless holds them, checked today. Where the sites disagree (Energia's 12 October price; Ecopower, whose own page shows 2024 prices), the truth follows the supplier's page. The Energia effect is shown above.
  • Plans only one site lists are left out of the error figures. bonkers.ie leaves out the current supplier's plans, so Pinergy (used as the current supplier) is missing from its results.
  • Gas, prepay and business were not tested. Peakless does not compare them in the same way.

What would close the remaining gaps in Peakless

  • Say plainly when a recommendation depends on letting the battery charge from the grid at night, and show the figure without it.
  • Ask one question about when a heat pump or storage heaters run, or nudge those homes towards the meter file, where Peakless is already within 1%.
  • Read a bill photo, as bonkers.ie does: the easiest way in for people without their meter file.
  • Add prepay plans for prepay homes, and two- and three-year costs.

Run 5 October 2026. bonkers.ie was used through its public comparison form in a browser, one session at a time with pauses; its data API, which its robots file bars, was not used. Homes, true costs, scripts and every raw result are kept with the Peakless repository in docs/deep-2026-10-05.
