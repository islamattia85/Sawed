Peakless vs bonkers.ie

Which is more accurate, easier and better for an Irish home choosing an electricity plan. Tested 5 October 2026 with three homes whose real yearly cost is known to the cent.
More accuratePeaklessWith a meter file, Peakless was within 0.2–0.8% of the true cost on average for every home and picked the cheapest plan each time. bonkers.ie missed the cheapest plan for one home and was 12.5% out on average for the EV home.
EasierPeakless, just3 taps from meter file to a full ranking, against 12 on bonkers.ie (plus downloading the file for either). Typed-in usage is close: 11 taps against 13.
Better overallDepends on the jobbonkers.ie lists more plans (Ecopower and a few others Peakless lacks), handles gas, switches you itself and is CRU-accredited. Peakless prices every hour, models solar, batteries and cars, and tells you why.

How it was tested

Three homes with a known answer. Each is a full year of half-hourly smart-meter readings in ESB Networks' own download format (the "HDF" file a customer gets from esbnetworks.ie). Because the readings are known, the true cost of every plan over the next 12 months can be worked out exactly: every half hour priced at the supplier's published rate for that hour, announced price changes from the date they start, the standing charge and the €6.67 PSO levy.

Read this table with care. With only a yearly figure, each site has to assume when the home uses its power. Peakless assumes ESB's standard home (Home 1's shape); bonkers.ie assumes ESB's Nightsaver home (Home 2's shape) for every smart meter. So each site scores almost perfectly on the home that matches its own assumption and worse on the other. That is not luck on either side: it shows the assumption matters more than anything else when no file is given. ESB's standard profile is what the market itself uses for a typical home without night storage heating, which is most homes, so Peakless's assumption fits more homes. But a night-heavy home that types in only a number gets a better answer from bonkers.ie.

What the test changed in Peakless

  • Meter-file bug, fixed. The first run had Peakless pick the wrong plan for the EV home, about €70 a year from the cheapest. ESB stamps each half hour with its end time; Peakless moved every reading back a whole hour, so half of them landed an hour early and overnight car charging slipped out of the cheap window. Fixed and covered by a new test; the results above are after the fix.
  • Earlier the same day, the first comparison had already found SSE's 5 October price rise (now in), Nightsaver plans offered to smart-meter homes (no longer), the missing urban/rural choice (added) and a hand-set usage curve (replaced by ESB Networks' standard profile).

Limits of this test

  • The homes are synthetic. They are built from ESB's own published profiles with day-to-day variation, not from real customers' meters. Real homes are messier, especially for the typed-usage test.
  • The "true" cost uses published prices as Peakless reads them. Every rate was checked against the supplier's page or matched bonkers.ie's, except Energia, whose page today says 27% off where bonkers.ie shows 23%. Where the two sites disagree on a price, the error is counted against bonkers.ie. If Energia's 12 October prices turn out as bonkers.ie shows, its Home 1 pick would be closer than shown here.
  • Plans only one site lists are left out of the error figures, as they cannot be checked against both.
  • Three homes, one area (urban), one day. Rural homes, solar homes and gas were not part of this test.

What would close the remaining gaps

  • Peakless: add Ecopower and the Energia and Yuno plans it lacks; check Electric Ireland Night Boost's standing charge; and, for typed usage only, ask one question about night use (a timed immersion, storage heating) so a night-heavy home is not priced as a typical one.
  • bonkers.ie (for the record): a typical smart-meter home is priced on the Nightsaver profile, which overstates night use; uploads are flagged as incomplete when they are not; and only 15 plans are shown after an upload.
